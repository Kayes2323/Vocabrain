# Module A — Parts of Speech

## A1. Function matters: identify the word's job in this sentence
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Word class বুঝুন sentence-এ শব্দটি কী কাজ করছে দেখে

A word's part of speech depends on how it functions in context. The same spelling can belong to different word classes:
- “I **answer** the question.” answer = verb.
- “I know the **answer**.” answer = noun.
- “They **clean** the room.” clean = verb.
- “The room is **clean**.” clean = adjective.
- “She works **hard**.” hard = adverb describing works.
- “It is a **hard** task.” hard = adjective describing task.

বাংলায় শুধু শব্দের বাংলা অর্থ জানলে word class সবসময় বোঝা যায় না। Sentence-এ শব্দটি noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, determiner, preposition বা conjunction হিসেবে কী ভূমিকা নিচ্ছে তা দেখুন।

### Core categories
- **Noun:** person, place, thing, concept (“student”, “Dhaka”, “progress”).
- **Verb:** action, event or state (“study”, “change”, “seem”).
- **Adjective:** describes a noun/pronoun (“useful information”, “the plan is useful”).
- **Adverb:** modifies a verb, adjective, another adverb or a whole clause (“works carefully”, “extremely useful”, “Fortunately, we arrived”).
- **Pronoun:** stands in for or refers to a noun phrase (“they”, “it”, “someone”).
- **Determiner:** helps specify a noun (“a”, “the”, “this”, “many”, “my”).
- **Preposition:** marks relationships (“in”, “under”, “during”).
- **Conjunction/linking word:** connects units or clauses (“and”, “but”, “although”); grammar labels vary by framework, so teach the function clearly.

### Common traps
- Not every word ending in -ly is an adverb (“friendly” is usually an adjective).
- Not every adjective comes before a noun (“The idea seems practical”).
- “Fast” can be adjective (“a fast car”) or adverb (“drive fast”).
- “Running” can be a verb form, noun-like gerund, or participial adjective depending on context.

### IELTS transfer
When editing Writing, identify the word's job before changing its form: “success” (noun), “successful” (adjective), “succeed” (verb), “successfully” (adverb). Word-family accuracy matters, but suffix alone is not a substitute for sentence analysis.

### Retrieval
Label the bold word and explain its function: “They **plan** carefully”; “Their **plan** is practical”; “The machine is **clean**”; “Please **clean** the machine.”

## A2. Word families and form choice
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Noun, verb, adjective ও adverb form সঠিকভাবে বাছা

A word family shares a root but the forms are not interchangeable:
- decide (verb), decision (noun), decisive (adjective), decisively (adverb)
- improve (verb), improvement (noun), improved/improving (verb forms or adjectives depending on context)
- economy (noun), economic (adjective), economically (adverb), economist (person)

### A practical decision
1. Find the slot: does the sentence need a noun, verb, adjective or adverb?
2. Check nearby grammar: articles/determiners often precede nouns; be/seem can be followed by adjectives; adverbs can modify verbs/adjectives.
3. Choose a natural collocation, not merely a form that exists.
4. Read the whole sentence to confirm meaning.

Examples:
- “There was a significant **improvement**.” Noun after an adjective and article.
- “The results **improved** significantly.” Verb + adverb.
- “The policy was **effective**.” Adjective after be.
- “The policy worked **effectively**.” Adverb describes worked.

### Common traps
- “The number increased significant.” → “increased significantly” (if describing how it increased).
- “This is a benefit solution.” → “This is a beneficial solution.”
- “We need to decision.” → “We need to decide” / “We need to make a decision.”
- Some forms change meaning or are not used in the same contexts; do not teach word-family tables as automatic substitution rules.

### IELTS transfer
Build vocabulary as a family plus example sentences. For every new word, save at least one natural collocation and one sentence; that is more useful than memorising translations alone.

### Retrieval
Complete: “The plan was ___ (success), and the team completed it ___ (efficient).” Explain why each form fits.

# Module B — Complex Sentences

## B1. Independent and dependent clauses
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Complex sentence মানে শুধু লম্বা sentence নয়

A complex sentence contains an independent clause and at least one dependent clause. A dependent clause has a subject and verb but cannot normally stand alone in that form because it depends on another clause or a subordinating word.

- Simple: “The bus arrived.”
- Compound: “The bus arrived, and we got on.”
- Complex: “When the bus arrived, we got on.”
- Complex: “We got on when the bus arrived.”

বাংলায়: sentence বড় হলেই complex হয় না। Clause-এর relationship দেখুন। “Because the bus was late” একা লিখলে সাধারণত fragment; “Because the bus was late, we missed the start” complete.

### Useful relationships
- Reason: because/since (“Because costs rose, fewer people bought it.”)
- Contrast/concession: although/even though/while (“Although it was costly, it was reliable.”)
- Time: when/after/before/as soon as (“After the class ended, we left.”)
- Condition: if/unless (“If the service improves, more people may use it.”)
- Relative clause: who/which/that (“The student who won the prize thanked her teacher.”)

### Punctuation and clause order
When a dependent clause comes first, a comma commonly separates it from the main clause: “Although the road was busy, we crossed carefully.” When it follows, a comma is often unnecessary: “We crossed carefully although the road was busy.” Punctuation may depend on emphasis and clause type; do not impose one punctuation pattern on every sentence.

### Common traps
- Fragment: “If the price falls.” → “If the price falls, demand may rise.”
- Double conjunction: “Because it was raining, so we stayed home.” → “Because it was raining, we stayed home” or “It was raining, so we stayed home.”
- Overlong sentences with several “which” clauses can hide the main point; split when clarity improves.

### IELTS transfer
Use complex sentences to express cause, contrast, condition and qualification precisely. A mixture of accurate simple and complex sentences is better than forcing complexity into every line. Complexity without control can make the argument harder to follow.

### Retrieval
Join “The service was expensive” and “Many people used it” in two different ways: one with “although”, one with “because” only if you can make the causal meaning logically clear.

## B2. Relative clauses: identify or add information
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Who, which, that দিয়ে noun সম্পর্কে তথ্য যোগ করুন

A relative clause modifies a noun or noun phrase:
- “The woman **who called yesterday** is my teacher.” The clause identifies/describes the woman.
- “The phone **that I bought** is already broken.”
- “My phone, **which I bought last year**, still works.” The comma-marked clause adds extra information about an already identified phone.

In many defining relative clauses, who/that can refer to people and which/that to things, but conventions vary by style. In non-defining clauses (extra information between commas), use who/which rather than that in standard formal writing. A relative pronoun can sometimes be omitted when it is the object (“The book (that) I read was useful”), but not when it is the subject (“The book that explains the rule…”).

বাংলায়: relative clause কোন noun-কে describe করছে, সেটি আগে খুঁজুন। Comma আছে কি নেই তা meaning-এর পার্থক্য তৈরি করতে পারে: clauseটি noun শনাক্ত করছে, নাকি extra information দিচ্ছে?

### Common traps
- “My brother, that lives in Dhaka, …” → in standard formal writing, “My brother, who lives in Dhaka, …”
- “The student who I met him…” → usually remove the redundant object: “The student who I met…”
- Do not add a comma automatically around every relative clause; defining and non-defining clauses work differently.

### IELTS transfer
Relative clauses can help combine related information, but keep the noun reference clear. If a sentence becomes hard to parse, split it into two.

### Retrieval
Explain the meaning difference: “Students who submitted the form received a message” and “The students, who submitted the form, received a message.” Context can affect interpretation; state the likely reading of each.

# Module C — Punctuation

## C1. Sentence boundaries: full stop, comma, semicolon
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Punctuation দিয়ে clause-এর boundary পরিষ্কার করুন

- **Full stop:** ends a complete declarative sentence.
- **Comma:** marks certain boundaries within a sentence, lists, introductory elements and non-defining clauses. It cannot normally join two independent clauses by itself.
- **Semicolon:** can join closely related independent clauses: “The sample was small; the results are therefore uncertain.”
- **Colon:** can introduce an explanation, list or elaboration after a complete clause: “The study had one limitation: its sample was small.”

বাংলায় comma যেখানে pause মনে হয় সেখানেই বসাবেন—এমন নিয়ম নেই। English punctuation grammatical structure এবং meaning দেখায়।

### Common traps
- Comma splice: “The sample was small, the results are uncertain.” → full stop, semicolon, or comma + conjunction.
- Fragment after colon: “The main problem was: because it was expensive.” Repair the grammar before choosing punctuation.
- Do not use a semicolon to join a dependent fragment to an independent clause.

### Retrieval
Repair “The policy is popular, it is also affordable” in two ways and explain the punctuation.

## C2. Apostrophes and quotation punctuation
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Apostrophe, possession আর contraction

- Contraction: “it’s” = “it is” or “it has”; “its” is possessive: “The company changed its policy.”
- Singular possession: “the student’s book”; plural possession: “the students’ books”; irregular plural: “the children’s books”.
- Plural nouns usually form the plural without an apostrophe: “three books”, not “three book’s”.
- Apostrophes in names ending in -s can vary by style guide; follow one consistent standard.
- Quotation punctuation conventions vary between British and American English. Follow the required style consistently; do not teach one country's convention as universal.

### Common traps
- “Its important” → “It’s important” (= it is).
- “The students’s results” → “the students’ results” (plural ending in -s).
- “1990’s” for a decade → “1990s” in standard usage.

### IELTS transfer
Apostrophe errors are avoidable editing errors. Check possessives and contractions, but in formal Writing, prefer full forms when they suit the register.

### Retrieval
Choose: “The company changed (its/it’s) website because (its/it’s) old design was confusing.” Explain each choice.

# Module D — Common Errors: repair the cause, not just the surface

## D1. Diagnose frequent sentence errors
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Common error ঠিক করতে আগে কারণ ধরুন

Use a repeatable correction routine:
1. Find the subject and finite verb.
2. Check tense and auxiliary.
3. Check the noun phrase (countability, article, number).
4. Check the connector and clause boundary.
5. Check collocation/preposition.
6. Re-read for intended meaning.

### Common patterns
- Subject–verb: “The list of items are long.” → “The list … is long.” (subject head = list)
- Auxiliary: “She doesn’t likes coffee.” → “She doesn’t like coffee.”
- Past question: “Did they arrived?” → “Did they arrive?”
- Countability: “many informations” → “much information” / “many pieces of information”.
- Article: “He is engineer.” → “He is an engineer.”
- Fragment: “Although the price increased.” → attach a main clause.
- Comma splice: “The price increased, sales fell.” → separate or use a suitable connector.
- Word form: “The plan worked successful.” → “The plan worked successfully.”
- Collocation: “depend of” → “depend on”.
- Redundancy: “return back” may be redundant in the intended meaning; “return” is usually enough.

বাংলায়: শুধু correct sentence মুখস্থ করবেন না—ভুলটি কেন হলো সেটিও শনাক্ত করুন। একই error pattern নতুন sentence-এ এলে তখন নিজে ঠিক করতে পারবেন।

### Avoid false rules
- A sentence may have more than one grammatical repair.
- Informal English and formal academic writing do not always use identical conventions.
- A phrase that is uncommon is not automatically grammatically wrong; judge it in context.
- Never change an answer key based only on a general rule if the original question has context that affects the answer.

### IELTS transfer
Make a personal error log with three columns: original sentence, corrected version, reason. Group repeated mistakes by cause (auxiliary, article, word form, clause boundary) and review them later. The goal is to recognise and prevent a pattern, not merely collect corrected sentences.

### Retrieval
Correct this sentence in at least two ways: “Although the number of student have increased, the school are still lack of teachers.” Explain every change and note any meaning you had to assume.
