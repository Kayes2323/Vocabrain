# Articles — curriculum map

Module 4 of the IELTS Foundation course (LEVEL 1 — Foundation Grammar).
Bangla has no articles, so the module teaches **meaning**, not a list of
rules: before every noun the student asks three questions.

> 1. Does the reader know exactly which one? → **the**
> 2. If not, is it one countable thing? → **a / an** (chosen by the sound)
> 3. Plural or uncountable, in general? → **no article**

**Lesson flow (v2, same as Tenses):** hook (the student answers first, with a
diagnosis for every option) → discover → when to use it and when NOT to
(with why Bangla speakers slip) → examples → IELTS in Reading, Listening,
Writing and Speaking → Common Mistake Lab → option practice → free recall
(no options) → error correction → mini challenge → a personal sentence Mino
checks (one follow-up question) → summary → spaced review.

Application lessons have no lesson concept; every question keeps the concept
of the article it tests, so each answer feeds the right review and mastery.

## Concepts, mastery and review

Four concepts: `article-a-an`, `article-a`, `article-the`, `article-zero`
(tag `article`). Each has a Mino-checked personal sentence, 5+ review questions,
free recall and mistake tracking. Mastery = recognition + recall + a correct
personal sentence + 2 spaced reviews **taken when due**.

## Mistake patterns

`missing-article`, `general-the`, `a-an-sound` (Articles page), and the shared
`noun-count` (a/an with uncountable nouns — also on Parts of Speech). Three in
14 days opens a 5-question fix at `/ielts/foundation/fix/<pattern>`, with a
rule before and "why it happens / how to recognise / how to avoid" after.

## Mino as a mentor

Article tasks add rules to the sentence check: name the noun and the one
question that decides the article; for a/an name the next word and how it
sounds; treat "an advice / a information" as its own fix; keep article errors
apart from other errors; one follow-up gap on the same decision.
Example: "I am student at an university" → "student" is one countable thing →
"a student"; "university" starts with a "yoo" sound → "a university";
follow-up: "My uncle is ___ engineer." (an).

## Articles Final Mastery Challenge

`/ielts/foundation/challenge/articles` (`content/articles-final.ts`): 6 parts —
a or an, one of many or the one, general or particular, free recall, fix the
sentence, IELTS Writing & Speaking — 4 items each at levels 1–3; 3 served per
part (18), adaptive. Report: overall %, by part, topic by topic, strongest and
weakest, the student's own mistakes, topics to review, recommended practice,
"Ask Mino about my Articles report". Stored in `foundation.finals.articles`.
It is a Mino learning assessment, not an official IELTS score.
