# Lesson sb-4 — Sentence types: statements, questions and commands
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Statement, question আর command চিনুন

### Start with meaning
Compare: “You need help.” / “Do you need help?” / “Please ask for help.” The basic message is related, but the speaker's purpose and word order change.

### Core idea
- **Statement:** gives information. “The library closes at six.”
- **Question:** asks for information. “Does the library close at six?”
- **Command/request:** tells or asks someone to do something. “Please close the window.”
- **Exclamation:** expresses strong feeling. “What a useful idea!”

বাংলায় sentence-এর ধরন শুধু punctuation দেখে আন্দাজ করবেন না; বাক্যটি কী কাজ করছে সেটাও বুঝুন। Standard direct questions often use an auxiliary before the subject (“Are you ready?” / “Do you work here?”). But indirect questions keep statement word order: “Could you tell me where the station is?”—not “where is the station” inside this sentence.

### Worked examples
1. “She works near the college.” Statement; subject before the verb.
2. “Where does she work?” Direct question; “does” carries tense, so the main verb stays “work”, not “works”.
3. “I wonder where she works.” Indirect question; no question-style inversion inside the clause.
4. “Please take a seat.” Request/command; the understood subject is “you”.

### Common traps
- Incorrect: “Where she works?” as a standard standalone direct question.  
  Better: “Where does she work?”  
  Why: the direct question needs the auxiliary “does” in this present-simple question.
- Incorrect: “Could you tell me where does she work?”  
  Better: “Could you tell me where she works?”  
  Why: the embedded question uses statement word order.
- Do not assume every question begins with a question word: “Are you ready?” is a yes/no question.

### IELTS transfer
In Speaking, practise answering the question actually asked. In Reading, distinguish a question quoted inside a text from the writer's own statement. In Writing, use question forms only when they suit the task and tone; an academic paragraph usually develops a point rather than asking a chain of rhetorical questions.

### Retrieval
Turn “They are studying online” into (1) a yes/no question and (2) a negative statement. Explain why the auxiliary changes position in the question.

# Lesson sb-5 — Positive, negative and question forms
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Positive, negative ও question form

### Start with a contrast
- “Rafi likes tea.”
- “Rafi does not like tea.”
- “Does Rafi like tea?”

Notice that when “does” appears, the main verb becomes “like”, not “likes”. The third-person singular ending is carried by the auxiliary.

### Core method
First identify the tense and the verb type. With **be**, put “not” after the be verb (“She is not ready”) and move it before the subject for a question (“Is she ready?”). With most present-simple and past-simple action verbs, use do/does/did for negatives and questions (“They did not arrive”; “Did they arrive?”). After do/does/did, use the base form of the main verb.

বাংলায়: “does/did” থাকলে main verb-এ আবার `-s` বা past form বসাবেন না। এক বাক্যে একই tense marker দুবার ব্যবহার করার ভুল এড়ান।

### Worked examples
- Statement: “He studies every evening.” Negative: “He does not study every evening.” Question: “Does he study every evening?”
- Statement: “They arrived early.” Negative: “They did not arrive early.” Question: “Did they arrive early?”
- Statement: “She is tired.” Negative: “She is not tired.” Question: “Is she tired?”
- Statement: “They can swim.” Negative: “They cannot swim.” Question: “Can they swim?” Modal verbs form negatives/questions without do-support.

### Common traps
- “Did you went?” → “Did you go?” The auxiliary marks past time.
- “She doesn’t likes it.” → “She doesn’t like it.”
- “Does he be busy?” → “Is he busy?” Use the correct form of “be”.
- “I no understand” → “I do not understand” in standard English.

### IELTS transfer
Use accurate tense and auxiliary forms when describing routines, past experiences and opinions in Speaking. During proofreading, search for combinations such as “did + past verb” and “doesn’t + verb-s”; these often reveal duplicated tense marking.

### Retrieval
Correct and explain: “Did Mina submitted the form?” Then make the corrected sentence negative.

# Lesson sb-6 — Word order: where each part belongs
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** English sentence-এ word order

### Start with meaning
“The student explained the answer” and “The answer explained the student” contain the same words, but their roles and meaning are different. English word order often signals who did what.

### Core idea
A common neutral statement pattern is **Subject + Verb + Object/Complement + other information**. This is a useful starting pattern, not a rule that every sentence must follow exactly. Adverbs and time/place phrases can move when emphasis or style calls for it, but the sentence must remain grammatical and clear.

বাংলায় word order তুলনামূলকভাবে flexible হতে পারে; তাই Bangla structure সরাসরি English-এ বসালে sentence অস্বাভাবিক হতে পারে। আগে main clause-এর subject ও verb ঠিক করুন, তারপর object এবং extra information বসান।

### Worked examples
- “The teacher explained the rule clearly.” Subject = the teacher; verb = explained; object = the rule; manner = clearly.
- “Yesterday, the teacher explained the rule.” The time phrase is fronted; the main clause remains grammatical.
- “The students are in the library.” “In the library” describes location; it is not a direct object.
- “She gave her brother a map.” Both “her brother” and “a map” follow the verb, with different roles.

### Common traps
- Incorrect: “Explained the teacher the rule.”  
  Better: “The teacher explained the rule.”  
  Why: a normal statement needs the subject in the expected position.
- Incorrect: “I every day practise English.”  
  More natural: “I practise English every day.” (Other placements may be possible with different emphasis.)
- Avoid translating every word in Bangla order; build the English clause first.

### IELTS transfer
For Writing, place the core claim early, then add evidence and conditions. For Reading, use word order to identify the actor and the affected person/thing. In Speaking, a clear basic sentence is preferable to a complex sentence whose word order you cannot control.

### Retrieval
Reorder these words into a natural sentence: “every weekend / the volunteers / clean / the beach”. Then identify subject, verb and object.

# Lesson sb-7 — Phrases and clauses: complete or dependent?
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Phrase আর clause-এর পার্থক্য

### Start with a test
Compare “after the class” with “after the class ended”. The first is a phrase: it has no subject–verb clause of its own. The second contains a subject (“the class”) and verb (“ended”), but because “after” makes it dependent, it usually needs a main clause to form a complete sentence.

### Core idea
- **Phrase:** a group of words functioning together but not containing a complete subject–verb clause.
- **Clause:** a unit built around a subject and a verb.
- **Independent clause:** can stand as a complete sentence in context.
- **Dependent clause:** has a subject and verb but begins with a word such as “because”, “although”, “when”, or “if” that makes it depend on a main clause.

বাংলায় clause-এর মধ্যে subject ও verb থাকতে পারে, তবু clause-টি একা complete sentence নাও হতে পারে। তাই “subject + verb আছে” দেখেই শেষ করবেন না—clauseটি independent, নাকি dependent, সেটাও দেখুন।

### Worked examples
- “During the exam” = phrase.
- “The exam ended” = independent clause.
- “When the exam ended” = dependent clause.
- “When the exam ended, the students left.” = dependent clause + independent clause.
- “The student who won the prize is my cousin.” The clause “who won the prize” describes “the student”; the main clause is “The student … is my cousin.”

### Common traps
- Fragment: “Although the bus was late.”  
  Complete: “Although the bus was late, we arrived on time.”
- Fragment: “The students waiting outside.”  
  Complete: “The students waiting outside have an appointment.” Here “waiting outside” is a participial phrase describing the students.
- Do not label every long group of words a clause; find its subject and verb.

### IELTS transfer
In Reading, bracket dependent clauses so the main claim is easier to see. In Writing, use dependent clauses to show cause, contrast or time—but attach them to a complete main clause.

### Retrieval
Label each as phrase, independent clause or dependent clause: “because the road was closed”; “near the entrance”; “the road was closed”.

# Lesson sb-8 — Join ideas without creating run-on sentences
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** দুইটি idea সঠিকভাবে যুক্ত করুন

### Start with a choice
Two facts: “The road was flooded.” “The match continued.” You can write two sentences, join them with a suitable connector, or use a semicolon in appropriate formal writing. You cannot simply put two independent clauses together with only a comma.

### Core patterns
- Separate sentences: “The road was flooded. The match continued.”
- Coordinating conjunction: “The road was flooded, but the match continued.”
- Cause/result: “The road was flooded, so the match was delayed.”
- Subordination: “Although the road was flooded, the match continued.”
- Semicolon between closely related independent clauses: “The road was flooded; the match was delayed.”

“Because” introduces a reason; “so” introduces a result. “Although” signals contrast and normally requires a main clause to complete the thought. Choose a connector based on the relationship, not just variety.

### Common traps
- **Comma splice:** “The road was flooded, the match was delayed.”  
  Repair: use a full stop, semicolon, or comma + suitable coordinating conjunction.
- **Run-on:** “The road was flooded the match was delayed.”  
  Repair: mark the boundary or add a grammatical connector.
- **Double connector:** “Although it rained, but we played.”  
  Better: “Although it rained, we played” OR “It rained, but we played.”
- A semicolon does not join a fragment to a complete sentence.

### IELTS transfer
In Writing Task 2, connectors should make the logical relationship clear. More linking words do not automatically improve cohesion; a wrong “therefore” can distort the argument. In Speaking, natural pauses and clear clauses often work better than forcing formal connectors into every sentence.

### Retrieval
Repair this sentence in two different ways: “The cost increased, people bought less.” Explain the relationship between the ideas in each version.

# Lesson sb-9 — Build and check a complete sentence
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** নিজের sentence তৈরি ও check করার পদ্ধতি

### The five-check routine
When you write a sentence, do not only ask “Does it sound right?” Use a repeatable check:
1. **Message:** What do I want to say?
2. **Core:** Where are the subject and main verb (or the structure of a question/command)?
3. **Completion:** Is this a full independent sentence, or have I left a dependent clause alone?
4. **Relationship:** If I joined ideas, does the connector express the right relationship and is punctuation correct?
5. **Read-back:** Read it once for meaning, then once for grammar.

বাংলায় আগে বক্তব্যটি ঠিক করুন, তারপর English structure বানান। শেষে নিজের sentence-কে ছোট ছোট অংশে ভেঙে দেখুন; শুধু বাংলা থেকে শব্দে-শব্দে অনুবাদ করবেন না।

### Worked repair
Draft: “Although many students wants to study abroad, they cannot afford it, they search for scholarships.”
1. Subject check: “many students” is plural, so use “want”, not “wants”.
2. Completion check: the “although” clause needs a main clause; “they cannot afford it” supplies one.
3. Boundary check: the final comma joins two independent clauses incorrectly.
4. Repair option A: “Although many students want to study abroad, they cannot afford it, so they search for scholarships.”
5. Repair option B: “Many students want to study abroad but cannot afford it, so they search for scholarships.”
The best version depends on the intended meaning; sentence repair is about communicating the relationship, not just adding punctuation.

### Common traps
- Editing punctuation before fixing the grammar of the clause.
- Making a sentence longer to sound academic, even when the relationship is unclear.
- Assuming there is only one correct rewrite. Many open-ended sentences have several grammatical repairs.

### IELTS transfer
Use this routine for Writing Task 1 descriptions and Task 2 arguments. In Speaking, use a lighter version: make one clear point, add a reason/example, and stop before losing control of the sentence. In Reading, reverse the process: locate the main clause, then interpret the added details.

### Retrieval
Write one sentence about a problem in your community. Check it using all five steps and explain one revision you made.
