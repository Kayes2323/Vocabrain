# Lesson sb-1 — What makes a complete sentence?
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** একটি sentence কখন সম্পূর্ণ হয়?
**Why it matters:** Complete sentences make Writing easier to follow and help you locate the main message in Reading. The goal is not to memorise a definition; it is to recognise whether a thought is grammatically complete.

### Start with a situation
You send a friend these messages:
- “The bus was late.”
- “Because the bus was late.”
- “The bus late.”

Which message makes a complete statement? The second makes the reader wait for the result; the third is missing a verb. The first communicates a complete thought.

### Build the mental model
A complete English sentence normally has a main clause: it tells us something about a subject, or gives a complete command/question. Subject and main verb are useful first clues, but do not turn this into the false rule that every sentence must show a subject explicitly. Commands such as “Wait here.” have an understood subject: you.

বাংলায় ভাবুন: sentence শুধু কয়েকটি শব্দ বা punctuation নয়। পাঠককে মূল বক্তব্যটি বুঝতে হবে। Subject খুঁজুন—কাকে/কী নিয়ে বলা হচ্ছে? তারপর main verb খুঁজুন—তার সম্পর্কে কী বলা হচ্ছে বা কী ঘটছে? শেষে দেখুন বক্তব্যটি কোনো dependent clause-এ আটকে গেছে কি না।

### Worked examples
1. **The cost of food increased last year.** Subject: “The cost of food”; main verb: “increased”. The statement is complete.
2. **Although the cost of food increased last year…** “Although” signals a contrast, but the contrast is not completed. The reader expects the main clause: “Although the cost of food increased last year, many families adapted.”
3. **My younger brother a university student.** The subject is present, but the linking verb is missing. Write: “My younger brother is a university student.”
4. **Please wait here.** Complete command. The subject “you” is understood, so it does not need to be written.

### Mistake lab
- **Fragment:** “Because public transport is cheap.”  
  **Repair:** “Many students use public transport because it is cheap.”  
  **Why:** “Because” introduces a reason; by itself, this clause leaves the main message unfinished.
- **Fragment:** “The people who live near the station.”  
  **Repair:** “The people who live near the station can walk to work.”  
  **Why:** The relative clause describes the people, but the sentence still needs to say something about them.
- **Misconception:** “If it starts with a capital and ends with a full stop, it is a sentence.”  
  **Correction:** Punctuation helps show boundaries; it cannot supply a missing main clause.

### IELTS transfer
**Writing:** Weak: “More people use buses. Because fuel is expensive.”  
Better: “More people use buses because fuel is expensive.”  
Also possible: “Fuel is expensive, so more people use buses.” The connector changes the relationship: “because” introduces the reason; “so” introduces the result.

**Reading:** In “Although the journey took longer than expected, the students arrived before the interview began,” find the main message first: **the students arrived**. The other clauses add contrast and timing.

**Speaking:** If you begin “Although I was tired…”, finish the contrast: “Although I was tired, I completed the assignment.” Short, complete sentences are a useful starting point; combine ideas when you can control the grammar.

### Retrieval
Without looking back, explain:
- What is the difference between a fragment and a complete sentence?
- Why is “Because I was busy.” usually incomplete as a standalone sentence?
- Why can “Close the door.” be complete without a written subject?

**Final check:** “If I have enough time.” is normally a fragment when used alone. One possible completion: “If I have enough time, I will call you.” Other completions can also be correct.

# Lesson sb-2 — Find the subject and the main verb
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Subject আর main verb কীভাবে খুঁজবেন?
**Why it matters:** Finding the grammatical core helps you understand long Reading sentences and catch missing-verb or agreement errors in Writing.

### Start with a trap
Read: “The number of students in the evening classes **has** increased.”
The nearest noun before “has” is “classes”, which is plural. Yet “has” is correct. Why? Because the grammatical subject is **the number**, not “classes”.

### The core idea
The subject is the person, thing, idea, or group the clause is about. The main verb tells what the subject does, what happens to it, or what state it is in. Do not choose the subject just by looking for the nearest noun.

বাংলায়: বাক্যে অনেক noun থাকতে পারে। Main verb-এর ঠিক আগের noun-টাই সব সময় subject নয়। Prepositional phrase বা relative clause-এর noun দেখে বিভ্রান্ত হবেন না।

### A reliable three-step method
1. Find the main verb or verb phrase.
2. Ask “Who or what does this clause make a statement about?” Find the subject that matches that verb.
3. Temporarily ignore extra detail between the subject and verb; then check whether the sentence still has a grammatical core.

### Worked examples
- “The students **in the library** are preparing for IELTS.” Subject = “The students”; “in the library” adds location; verb phrase = “are preparing”.
- “A list of recommended books **is** available online.” Subject = “A list”; “of recommended books” tells us what kind of list.
- “The report that the researchers published **was** widely discussed.” Main subject = “The report”; “that the researchers published” describes the report.
- “There **are** several reasons for the change.” This is an existential “there” construction. Do not apply a simplistic “there is always singular” rule; the verb agrees with the following subject in standard usage.

### Mistake lab
- Incorrect: “The quality of the roads **have** improved.”  
  Better: “The quality of the roads **has** improved.”  
  Reason: subject head = “quality” (singular); “of the roads” is extra information.
- Incorrect analysis: In “Students who work part-time often feel tired,” treating “work” as the main verb of the whole sentence.  
  Better analysis: “who work part-time” is a relative clause; the main clause is “Students … feel tired.”

### IELTS transfer
When Reading sentences feel long, do not translate every word first. Mark the main subject and verb, then add the extra clauses back in. In Writing, use the same method to check whether a long subject has the correct verb.

### Retrieval
Explain why “A group of students is waiting” uses “is” in this context. Then create your own sentence with a long subject and underline its main verb.

# Lesson sb-3 — Build a clear sentence: subject, verb, and object
**বাংলা শিরোনাম:** Subject, verb আর object দিয়ে পরিষ্কার sentence
**Why it matters:** Recognising sentence roles helps you build clear statements and understand who did what to whom. Not every sentence needs an object.

### Start with meaning
Compare:
- “The researcher analysed the results.”
- “The results analysed the researcher.”
Both contain similar words, but the meaning changes because the subject and object roles change.

### Understand the roles
- **Subject:** who/what the clause is about, and often the doer of an action.
- **Verb:** the action, event, or state.
- **Object:** the person or thing affected by the action, when the verb takes an object.
- **Complement:** information that completes the meaning of a linking verb, as in “The result was surprising.”

বাংলা মনে রাখার কৌশল: শুধু word-এর position দেখে সবসময় role ঠিক করবেন না; verb-এর meaning-ও বুঝতে হবে। তবে English-এর সাধারণ statement-এ Subject + Verb + Object একটি খুব সাধারণ pattern।

### Contrast set
- “Prices increased.” Subject + verb; no object is needed.
- “The council increased bus fares.” Subject + verb + object.
- “The buses are crowded.” “Crowded” describes the subject after the linking verb “are”; it is not an object.
- “Mina gave her friend a notebook.” “Her friend” is the indirect object; “a notebook” is the direct object.
- “The survey was completed by students.” This is passive voice. “The survey” is the grammatical subject even though students performed the action.

### Worked reasoning
In “The government introduced a new policy last year,” first find the verb: “introduced”. Ask who introduced it: “The government” = subject. Ask what it introduced: “a new policy” = object. “Last year” tells when; it is not the object.

### Mistake lab
- “The city became rapidly.” If the intended meaning is that the city changed quickly, write “The city changed rapidly.” If describing its state, write “The city became crowded.” After linking verbs such as “become”, an adjective often describes the subject.
- “The number of visitors increased tourism.” This likely mixes two structures. Either “The number of visitors increased” or “The increase in visitors boosted tourism.” Choose the sentence that expresses your intended meaning.

### IELTS transfer
**Writing Task 1:** “The percentage of households using solar power increased steadily.” Subject = “The percentage”; verb = “increased”; “steadily” tells how.  
**Speaking:** “I enjoy reading because it helps me relax.” Main clause = “I enjoy reading”; the reason clause explains why.  
**Reading:** In passive sentences, focus on grammatical roles and meaning, not only on who appears first.

### Retrieval
For “The new policy made public transport more affordable,” identify the subject, verb, object, and the phrase that describes the object. Then write one sentence with no object and one with an object.
