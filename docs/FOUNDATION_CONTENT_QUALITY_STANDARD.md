# Mino Foundation Content Quality Standard

**Status:** Pilot standard — apply to one lesson, review, then scale.
**Audience:** IELTS learners, especially Bangla-speaking learners, with English examples preserved.
**Product promise:** A learner should leave each lesson understanding an idea, knowing when and why to use it, and being able to apply it independently—not merely remembering a definition.

## 1. Non-negotiable principles

1. **Teach understanding, not page length.** Longer content is not automatically better. Every paragraph must explain, distinguish, demonstrate, diagnose a misconception, or help the learner apply the concept.
2. **Explain the why and the how.** Define the idea, show why it works, show how to recognise it, and explain the decision process.
3. **Make thinking visible.** Worked examples must narrate the reasoning step by step, not only show the correct answer.
4. **Contrast near-misses.** Include examples that look plausible but fail, with a precise explanation of why.
5. **Use natural, useful English.** Examples should sound like something a real person would say or write. Avoid awkward textbook sentences and fake “IELTS tricks.”
6. **Use Bangla to clarify, not replace English exposure.** Explain difficult ideas in natural Bangla, retain key English terms, and show the actual English forms learners need to understand or produce.
7. **Connect to IELTS only when relevant.** Explain transfer to Reading, Listening, Writing, or Speaking concretely. Do not force an IELTS label onto every concept.
8. **Practice must follow teaching.** Practice should test what was taught, progressively remove scaffolding, and include useful feedback for wrong answers.
9. **Be honest about rules and exceptions.** Avoid claims like “always” unless they are genuinely always true. Flag context, register, dialect, and accepted variants where relevant.
10. **No fake personalization or unsupported claims.** Do not claim a lesson guarantees a band score or that an examiner always penalizes something in a particular way without a reliable basis.

## 2. Recommended lesson experience

Choose the elements that suit the concept; this is a flexible teaching sequence, not a rigid template.

1. **Hook / diagnostic:** A short puzzle, common mistake, relatable situation, or prediction question that creates a reason to learn.
2. **Intuition first:** Build a mental model using a familiar example before presenting terminology.
3. **Core explanation:** State the concept accurately; unpack why it works, how to identify it, and how it differs from related concepts.
4. **Worked example:** Walk through the decision in explicit steps. Explain both why the correct choice works and why plausible alternatives fail.
5. **Contrast set:** Compare minimal pairs or correct/incorrect examples. Change one feature at a time where possible.
6. **Common misconceptions:** Name the mistake, explain why it is tempting, and give a correction strategy.
7. **Guided practice:** The learner answers with hints or partial reasoning.
8. **Independent practice:** Remove hints; vary contexts and question formats.
9. **Transfer:** Apply the concept in realistic IELTS Reading, Listening, Writing, or Speaking contexts when appropriate.
10. **Recall / exit check:** Ask the learner to explain the rule in their own words or solve one new example. Keep the takeaway short.
11. **Review cue:** Identify what should be revisited later based on evidence of learning, not simply because the lesson ended.

Not every lesson needs all 11 parts. Do not force a story, joke, IELTS example, or visual where it adds no teaching value.

## 3. Explanation depth checklist

A lesson is not ready until the author has considered:

- What misconception or practical problem makes this concept difficult?
- What should the learner understand before seeing the formal rule?
- What is the rule, in accurate plain language?
- Why does the rule work, or what pattern can the learner observe?
- How can a learner identify the concept in a new example?
- What is the step-by-step decision procedure?
- What is a common wrong answer, and why might a learner choose it?
- What are the limits, exceptions, or contextual differences?
- Can a learner transfer the idea to a new example without copying the model?
- Does the lesson teach the concept before assessing it?

If a topic cannot reasonably answer “why,” explain the relevant pattern, convention, or limitation rather than inventing a reason.

## 4. Bilingual writing standard

- Use natural contemporary Bangla, not word-for-word translation from English.
- Keep important English terms visible, then explain them simply in Bangla on first use.
- Do not mix English into every sentence merely to sound modern. Use English where it is the target language or the term is genuinely useful.
- Explain the English example in context. Do not translate so literally that the learner misses the grammar or intended meaning.
- Use consistent terms across lessons (subject, verb, clause, phrase, tense, article, etc.).
- Avoid patronizing language, filler, exaggerated hype, and shaming learners for mistakes.
- Where useful, include a quick Bangla gloss, but the learner must still inspect and practise the English.

## 5. Examples and practice quality

- Use realistic contexts relevant to students' lives and broad international settings; do not make every example about studying abroad.
- Include affirmative, negative, and question forms where the concept calls for them.
- Include both clear cases and carefully chosen edge cases.
- Vary names, places, topics, and sentence structures; do not reuse one model with only a noun swapped.
- Distractors should reveal a real misconception, not be silly or obviously wrong.
- Explanations for answers must teach a transferable reason, not merely restate the answer.
- For open-ended answers, use explicit rubrics/checklists and accept legitimate variants.
- Preserve exact-answer validation where IELTS task instructions require exact words from a passage or a specific response format. Do not loosen answer checking globally just to make learners feel successful.
- Ensure every practice item is supported by the lesson content and every important teaching point receives a meaningful check.
- Remove duplicate, ambiguous, ungrammatical, culturally confusing, or factually inaccurate questions.

## 6. Visuals and interaction

Use visuals only when they improve comprehension. Useful options include:
- colour-coded sentence anatomy;
- timelines for tense/aspect;
- side-by-side minimal pairs;
- clause-bracketing or sentence trees;
- step-by-step reveal of a worked example;
- prediction questions before explanation;
- hint ladders that reveal one clue at a time.

Do not require an image or interaction if the existing product schema cannot render it. In that case, write a clear text-based alternative and separately document the component needed. Never promise a visualization that is not implemented.

## 7. Editorial and factual review

For each lesson, review:
- grammar accuracy and naturalness of every English example;
- Bangla explanation clarity;
- terminology consistency;
- validity of answer keys and accepted answers;
- alignment between explanation and practice;
- claims about IELTS format, scoring, examiner behaviour, or official rules against current authoritative sources;
- accessibility and readability on mobile;
- suitability for the stated learner level.

For disputed usage, note that English varies by context and register; do not teach a preference as a universal rule.

## 8. Quality rubric (score each dimension 0–4)

- **Accuracy:** 0 = wrong; 2 = mostly right but incomplete; 4 = accurate with relevant limits explained.
- **Depth:** 0 = definition only; 2 = some explanation; 4 = why/how/recognition/transfer are clear.
- **Clarity:** 0 = confusing; 2 = understandable with effort; 4 = clear on first read for the target learner.
- **Examples:** 0 = absent/wrong; 2 = limited/repetitive; 4 = natural, varied, contrasting, and diagnostic.
- **Practice alignment:** 0 = unrelated or broken; 2 = partial coverage; 4 = progression from guided to independent with meaningful feedback.
- **Bilingual quality:** 0 = awkward/misleading; 2 = understandable but uneven; 4 = natural Bangla supports accurate English learning.
- **Engagement:** 0 = monotonous; 2 = some variety; 4 = purposeful curiosity, interaction, and a satisfying learning arc.
- **Transfer:** 0 = no application; 2 = generic application; 4 = learner can use the idea in a new relevant context.

**Release gate:** Target at least 26/32, with no score below 3 for Accuracy, Practice alignment, or Bilingual quality. Any factual error, invalid answer key, or misleading rule blocks release regardless of score. Score is an editorial tool, not a claim that a learner will achieve a particular result.

## 9. Implementation safety

- Audit the existing lesson model and renderer before adding new step kinds or fields.
- Preserve lesson IDs, exercise IDs, progress compatibility, answer-validation semantics, and locale structure unless a deliberate migration is separately reviewed.
- Do not rewrite the entire Foundation in one batch.
- Pilot one lesson, review it, then a small representative set before scaling.
- Keep a change log and track unresolved questions.
- Run relevant unit tests, type checks, build, and a regression check for existing progress and practice.
- Do not claim a lesson or feature is live until it is merged and verified in the deployed app.

## 10. Learner-value test

After a pilot lesson, ask a learner to:
1. explain the concept in their own words;
2. solve a new example they have not seen;
3. explain why a plausible wrong answer is wrong;
4. say which part still feels unclear.

Track completion, answer accuracy, hint use, return-to-review performance, and qualitative feedback. High completion or long reading time alone does not prove learning or willingness to pay. Subscription value must be earned through demonstrable clarity, reliable practice, progress, and trust—not exaggerated marketing.
