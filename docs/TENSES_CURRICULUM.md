# Tenses for IELTS — curriculum map

Module 2 of the IELTS Foundation course. Tenses are taught through **time**,
not as a list of 12 tenses to memorise. Every lesson answers six questions:

> When did it happen? · Is it finished? · Is it happening now? · Is it
> repeated? · Did it continue? · Does it connect to now? · Is it planned?

**Lesson flow (v2 format):** Hook (the student answers first) → Diagnose
("if you chose X, here's why") → Discover (notice the pattern) → Explain →
Real life → IELTS connection (only where natural) → Common Mistake Lab →
Practice (easy → hard) → Active recall (no options) → Personal use (Mino
feedback) → Remember → spaced review (same day, 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days).

**Mastery of a concept** is not "lesson completed". It needs:
recognition (≥ 80% on choice questions, 3+ answers), recall (2+ correct
typed answers), application (1 personal sentence Mino judged correct) and
consistency (2 spaced reviews passed, each taken when it was due — repeating a
review early is practice and does not count).

## Every lesson (v2)

Hook → discover → when to use it **and when not to** (with the reason Bangla
speakers slip) → examples → IELTS use in all four skills (Reading, Listening,
Writing, Speaking) → common mistakes → option practice → free recall (no
options) → error correction → mini challenge → a personal sentence checked by
Mino (one follow-up question) → summary. Lessons 11–14 are application
lessons: their questions keep the concept of the tense they test, so every
answer feeds the right tense's mastery and review.

## Concepts, mastery and review

Nine tense concepts: time, present-simple, present-continuous, past-simple,
past-continuous, present-perfect, present-perfect-continuous, past-perfect,
future. Every one has a Mino-checked personal sentence (application), 5+
review questions, recall questions and mistake tracking, so every one can
reach real mastery. Nothing is marked mastered for finishing a lesson.

## Tense mistake patterns

Same mistake system as Parts of Speech (`content/pos-patterns.ts`):
`past-vs-perfect` (Past Simple or Present Perfect), `simple-vs-continuous`,
`tense-time` (the time word decides the tense), plus the shared `verb-form`
and `sv-agreement`. Three in 14 days opens a 5-question fix
(`/ielts/foundation/fix/<pattern>`) on the Tenses page.

## Mino as a mentor

For tense tasks the sentence check gets extra rules: name the time word or
context that decides the tense, keep a wrong tense choice apart from a wrong
verb form, and give one follow-up gap on the same decision. Example: "I have
went to Dhaka yesterday" → 'yesterday' is a finished time, so Past Simple
('went'); separately, 'have went' is never a correct form ('went' / 'have
gone'); follow-up: "Last week we ___ (visit) Sylhet."

## Tenses Final Mastery Challenge

`/ielts/foundation/challenge/tenses` (`content/tenses-final.ts`): 8 parts —
identify the time, choose from context, correct the verb, free recall,
explain, Reading & Listening, IELTS Writing, Speaking & building sentences —
4 items each at levels 1–3; 3 served per part (24), adaptive (right → harder,
wrong → easier). Report: overall %, by part, tense by tense, strongest and
weakest tense, the student's own mistakes, tenses to review, recommended
practice, "Ask Mino about my Tenses report". Stored in `foundation.finals.tenses`.
It is a Vocab Brain learning assessment, not an official IELTS score.
